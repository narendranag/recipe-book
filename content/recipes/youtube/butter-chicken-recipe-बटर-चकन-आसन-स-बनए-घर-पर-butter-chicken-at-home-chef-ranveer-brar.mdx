ingredients:
- 4 large tomatoes, halved
- 2-3 large onions, sliced
- 3-4 garlic cloves
- 1 inch ginger, sliced
- 1 tbsp Degi Mirch (Kashmiri chili powder)
- 5-6 cloves
- 1 inch cinnamon stick
- 3 bay leaves
- 5-6 black peppercorns
- 2 green cardamom pods
- 3½ tbsp butter, divided
- Salt to taste
- 1 tbsp red chili powder
- 1 tsp coriander powder
- 3 tbsp fresh cream, plus more for garnish
- 1 tsp honey
- 2 cups cooked tandoori chicken, shredded
- 1-2 drops kewra water
- 1 tbsp dried fenugreek leaves, toasted and crushed
- Burnt charcoal
- 1 tsp ghee
- Fresh coriander sprig, for garnish
instructions:
- 'To make the base gravy: In a heavy-bottomed pan, add ½ cup water.'
- Add tomatoes, onions, garlic, ginger, Degi Mirch, and all whole spices (cloves,
  cinnamon, bay leaves, peppercorns, and cardamom). Mix well.
- Add 1½ tsp butter and salt, then mix well. Cover and cook for 15 minutes until tomatoes
  are soft.
- Using a hand blender, blend the gravy until completely smooth.
- Strain the gravy through a fine strainer to remove solids.
- In a clean pan, melt 2 tbsp butter over medium heat. Add red chili powder and coriander
  powder, cooking for about 1 minute.
- Pour in the prepared gravy and mix well. Cook for 2-3 minutes.
- Add fresh cream, honey, and shredded tandoori chicken. Mix well and cook for another
  3-4 minutes.
- Stir in kewra water and dried fenugreek leaves. Cook for 2 more minutes.
- 'For the smoky finish: Place burnt charcoal in a small metal bowl and position it
  in the center of the gravy. Pour ghee over the charcoal, immediately cover with
  a lid, and keep covered for 2-3 minutes.'
- Remove the charcoal bowl and discard.
- Transfer butter chicken to a serving bowl. Garnish with fresh cream and a coriander
  sprig. Serve hot with roti or rice.
notes: The burnt charcoal infuses the dish with a subtle smoky flavor—this is an optional
  but authentic touch. Make sure to strain the gravy thoroughly for a smooth consistency.
  Tandoori chicken can be prepared ahead of time for convenience.
